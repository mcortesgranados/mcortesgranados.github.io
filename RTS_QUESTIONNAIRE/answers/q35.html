Have you worked on systems involving distributed transactions?

DistributedTransactionsArchitecture

✅ Yes — I have designed and implemented consistency across multiple services and databases, using saga-style coordination rather than fragile two-phase commit.

Technical detail

  • 🔄 Saga pattern — orchestration (a coordinator/handler drives the steps) and choreography (services react to events) to model long-running business processes such as payments, reservations, order fulfilment and claims, each step paired with an explicit compensating action.
  • 📤 Transactional Outbox — business data and outgoing events committed in the same local transaction, then published to Kafka/RabbitMQ/Service Bus by a relay; guarantees no lost or phantom events without distributed locks.
  • 📥 Idempotency and exactly-once effects — idempotency keys, deduplication tables, unique constraints and conditional writes so retried or duplicated messages (at-least-once delivery) never double-charge or double-create.
  • 🧪 Consistency engineering — eventual consistency with read-your-writes handling where UX demands it, reconciliation/repair jobs, timeouts and compensating timeouts, poison-message handling with DLQs, and distributed tracing to follow a business transaction across services.
  • ⚖️ When 2PC still matters — inside a single transactional resource (one database, or TransactionScope over tightly coupled resources) I use local ACID transactions; across service boundaries I replace locking with compensation and idempotency because 2PC does not scale and couples availability.
🧾 CV evidence: CV: AWS Event Driven Architectural Microservice, MQ Systems (Kafka, RabbitMQ, ActiveMQ), Oracle SOA Suite/BPEL orchestration and BPEL/BPM process services in banking and government integrations.
