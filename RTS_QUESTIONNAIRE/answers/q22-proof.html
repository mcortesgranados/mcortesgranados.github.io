22. Responsive UIs across viewport sizes

Answer as submitted

“Yes — mobile, tablet, laptop and desktop, validated for behaviour, a11y and speed.”
Technical decision: expand. The submitted line states the viewports but not what changes at each one or how it is proven. This page is the separate, detailed version: it shows the concrete requirements per class and the matrix that verifies them in CI.

Expanded answer

Yes — responsive delivery is a baseline in all my UI work, and I treat it as behaviour rather than only styling. In practice: mobile (320–480 px) single column, tap targets of at least 44 px, bottom-sheet navigation, tables transformed into cards and virtualised lists; tablet (601–1024 px) two columns with navigation that becomes persistent; laptop (1025–1599 px) the full shell with side navigation, real data-table columns and density mode; desktop/wide (1600 px+) a capped measure instead of stretched content, with container queries so components adapt to their slot rather than to the viewport. Everything is verified with a viewport matrix in CI plus accessibility and performance budgets, and touch, hover, orientation and reduced-motion preferences are all handled.

How responsive work is kept honest

Rules I apply. Design mobile-first and let the layout grow; use a named breakpoint scale instead of magic numbers; cap the measure on wide screens rather than stretching text; make components adapt to their container with container queries; never force a horizontal scroll on a table; keep 44 px touch targets everywhere; and verify the whole matrix in CI with accessibility and performance budgets rather than checking one device by hand.
