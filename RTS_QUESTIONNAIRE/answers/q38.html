Have you worked with asynchronous/background processing or job-processing frameworks?

Background JobsAsyncMessaging

✅ Yes — asynchronous and scheduled processing is a recurring part of my designs on .NET, Java, Python and cloud platforms.

Technical detail

  • 🟪 .NET — IHostedService/BackgroundService workers, Channel<T>-based producer/consumer queues, Hangfire/Quartz.NET-style schedulers for recurring and delayed jobs, and IHttpClientFactory + Polly for resilient outbound calls.
  • ☕ Java/Spring — @Async/@Scheduled tasks, thread-pool executors, Spring Batch/JMS listeners and Kafka consumers with consumer groups, offset management and rebalance handling.
  • ☁️ Cloud-native — AWS Lambda triggered by S3/SQS/EventBridge/CloudWatch Events for scheduled reports, backups and maintenance; Step Functions for multi-step workflows with retries; Azure Functions, Service Bus queues/topics and Logic Apps; Airflow DAGs orchestrating ETL pipelines.
  • 🐍 Python — Celery-style task queues, APScheduler/FastAPI background tasks, and batch data jobs for ETL and reporting.
  • 🛡️ Reliability patterns — idempotent job handlers, at-least-once delivery with deduplication, retry with exponential backoff and jitter, dead-letter queues and poison-message handling, visibility timeouts, job observability (duration, failure rate, backlog) and graceful shutdown during deploys.

🎯 Design rule: anything that can exceed a request budget — emails, PDF generation, exports, third-party sync, ETL, AI inference — is moved to the background with status tracking surfaced back to the UI.
