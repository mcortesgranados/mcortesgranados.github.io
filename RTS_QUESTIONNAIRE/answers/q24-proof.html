24. Angular concepts in practice

Answer as submitted

“Yes — all of these are part of my everyday Angular practice, not occasional exposure.”
Technical decision: expand. The submitted line claims fluency but shows nothing. This page is the separate, detailed version: it takes the concepts the question lists and shows how each is implemented, plus the effect on bundle size, navigation, network behaviour and form reliability.

Expanded answer

All of them are part of everyday practice. Lazy loading by route boundary with preloading for the next likely area and bundle budgets in CI. Guards and resolvers (canActivate, canMatch, canDeactivate) so authorisation and data are decided before a component is created. Functional interceptors for token attachment, single refresh-and-replay on 401, retries limited to idempotent calls, and a correlation id on every request. RxJS composition with the operator that matches the semantics (switchMap for cancellable reads, combineLatest for derived views, takeUntilDestroyed for teardown) alongside signals for local state and a store for genuinely shared state. Reusable components with typed inputs/outputs and content projection, custom directives and pipes for cross-cutting presentation, and typed reactive forms with cross-field and debounced async validators.

Why the concepts earn their place

Rules I apply. Split at the route boundary and budget the result; decide authorisation before a component exists; resolve data so nothing renders empty; keep auth, retry and tracing in interceptors rather than in every service; choose the flattening operator for the semantics you actually need and tear streams down with the lifecycle; keep local state local and share only what is truly shared; and make forms typed and validated on both sides. These are conventions a team can review, which is what makes them durable.
